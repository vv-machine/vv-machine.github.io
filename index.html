VV-machine

Veera Vartiainen, working under the name VV-machine, is a Helsinki-based artist whose practice moves between surreal vector systems, physical materials and site-specific installations.

Her work combines digital methods with recycled, ecological and natural materials to create pieces that shift between image, object and environment. A recurring foundation in her practice is the ongoing series Vector Meditation, from which she has developed editions, murals and installations in different contexts.

Her projects often explore the relationship between technological imagery, embodied experience and the material world through a modular visual language that continues to transform across formats.

Vector Meditation Modular digital visual art series (2016 – ongoing)

(Current) Technique and Materials: scalable vector art, hand-mounted on recycled plywood, sealed with eco-branded resin, finished with hand-painted details
Edition: each core piece exists as a limited edition of 15 variations, custom size, colour and shape
Selected exhibitions: Asbestos Art Space, Hirvitalo, MUU Ikkuna, Purkutaide, Kosmos Festival, Yö Galleria, Aava taidepolku, Kunsthaus Jägermeister, Arctic15

Vector Meditation is built around six primary artworks. Each one is repeated in different colours, sizes and materials using vector technology, in a surreal pop art-influenced process. The same base image is transformed through different materials and methods, creating limited variations rather than identical copies.

The series explores how far vector-based image making can be pushed into physical, ecological and repurposed materials. The techniques and materials keep changing from edition to edition as I look for more sustainable ways to produce the works, from laser-cut plywood and natural pigments to hand-painted and assembled surfaces.

The imagery combines surreal and visionary elements with a modular visual language. For me, these kinds of images are a way of making visible things that cannot always be represented through realistic imagery: instinct, imagination, subconscious associations and ways of experiencing reality that exist outside the immediately familiar. Surreal and visionary images create space for these other ways of seeing, especially in a visual culture increasingly dominated by functional, commercial and easily recognisable imagery.

The visual language of Vector Meditation has also developed into site-specific installations, wall works, textiles and applied collaborations.

Lost in Connection Site-specific immersive installation

Size: approx. 5 m²
Materials and techniques: vector art, animation, Pepper's Ghost hologram, Google Forms, laser cutting, wall paint, recycled electronics, found materials, recycled paints, recycled plywood, thin plywood
Selected through open call: Purkutaide 2025 Parempi Maailma exhibition.

The starting point is the crisis of binary thinking: opposites that could complement one another instead turn against each other.

The installation is the resolution of giving in to this polarised way of communicating, situated just after the end of the world and just before a new beginning.

We can still decide which one.

The work is based on the modular visual language of the Vector Meditation series.

End of the World, or a New Beginning?

Visitors could take part in the installation directly through a Google Form, accessed via QR-code stickers placed around the space, evaluating their own experience of the end of the world versus a new beginning.

Portal of Dataomes Site-specific environmental installation

Materials and techniques: natural pigments, thin plywood, jute rope, vector art and laser cutting.

Commissioned by: Kunsthaus Jägermeister

Exhibitions: Solstice Festival 2024 and Aava in 2025.

Portal of Dataomes is a site-specific environmental installation exploring the non-genetic information carried within us and its connection to the surrounding world.

The work moves between the cosmic and the earthly, opening a visual portal through which the viewer can reflect on the intertwined relationship between humanity, technology and nature.

Stairway to Home I–III Commissioned mural series

2025

Materials and techniques: leftover paint, natural pigments, cardboard, vector art and laser cutting.
Commissioned by: Asunto Oy Vesilinnankatu 25

Stairway to Home I–III is a three-part mural commission for the stairwells of a residential apartment building at Vesilinnankatu 25. The designs were developed together with the building's residents, responding to the Alppila area's character, the building's 1930s architecture, and the existing colour palette. Designed digitally as vector art, then converted to stencils and painted by hand using self-mixed leftover paints.

Mermaid Slaughterhouse Multidisciplinary Art Collective (Ecological visual art, performance, site-specific installations)

2024 - ongoing

Mermaid Slaughterhouse is an art collective formed by Mira Niittymäki, Loviisa Lassila and VV-machine. The collective works to dismantle stereotypes related to femininity and traditional crafts through contemporary art.

Their practice highlights passion, cultural creativity, and holistic interpretations of nature, drawing connections between ancient mythology, contemporary art, and science. Mermaid Slaughterhouse works with natural materials traditionally associated with craft, such as fish leather and willow, and brings them into modern contexts using digital tools like laser cutting.

The collective unapologetically elevates traditional craft materials into contemporary art, while exploring the communal nature of making. Their work is grounded in grassroots feminism and ecological thinking, where the personal is understood as political.

Syli (Hug)

Materials and Techniques: self-made fish leather, clay paint, laser-cut finnish birch plywood

Exhibitions: Kosmos Festival 2024, Aava 2024 and Arctic15

A site-specific, multidisciplinary installation combining visual art and performance. The work explores the microscopic layers of the forest biosphere and the smallness of the individual within the networks of plants and fungi.

The installation also functions as a stage for Loviisa Lassila's performance, where her alter ego Polina Wolfgang tells the story of a wolf child.

Virta (Flow)

Materials and Techniques: self-made fish leather, clay paint, local natural pigments, willow, red clay, fish trap, laser-cut finnish birch plywood

Exhibitions: Kosmos Festival 2025 and Aava 2025

Virta examines and visualizes the structures and details of a lake ecosystem, from its deepest layers to the whole. The work merges scientific and mythological imagery into a unified visual language.

Henki (Spirit)

Materials and Techniques: self-made fish leather, clay paint, local natural pigments, laser-cut finnish birch plywood

Exhibitions: Mikro-Kosmos 2026

Henki (The Spirit) is an installation combining two collections: Syli (The Hug) and Virta (The Flow).

Bringing these two environments together, Henki (The Spirit) questions whether the forest and the lake, or the human soul and how it understands itself, should be understood as separate entities at all. We are all part of one large, messy web, but still we try to find and create entities and boundaries wherever we choose to see them. By trying to make the world simpler through definition, we end up making it more complex.

Itämeren väki

Itämeren väki is Mermaid Slaughterhouse collective’s performative community art project that invites feminine power to protect the Baltic Sea. In Finnish tradition, the female genitalia has been regarded as a gateway to the otherworld and believed to contain great spiritual power, known as väki. Through this power, women have been able to protect things through a ritual practice known as harakoiminen.

Thank You For Not Toxic Wasting Site-specific sculpture made from locally collected cigarette butts - 2022

Size: 35cm x 50cm x 25cm
Materials and techniques: cigarette butts, chicken wire, paper mache, wood glue
Exhibited: S.A.V Street Art Triennial, Kunsthaus Jägermeister, Yö Galleria.

S.A.V (Street Art Vantaa) invited VV-machine to take part in Myyrmäki's first Street Art Triennial, an event that will now be held every three years. The first edition's theme addressed the temporary nature of street art and ecological approaches to making it.

Thank You For Not Toxic Wasting is made from cigarette butts collected in Myyrmäki. Cigarette butts are the most common form of litter in the world, and one of the biggest contributors to ocean pollution.
